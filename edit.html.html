01 Overview

This analysis covers 1,680 acquisition records across five primary departments. Architecture & Design dominates at 71% of all records (1,192), followed by Photography at 17% (278), Drawings & Prints at 6% (104), Painting & Sculpture at 3% (51), and Media and Performance at 2% (33).


A critical finding is that 2015 entry data is highly misleading: of the 1,020 records entered that year, 836 (84%) were actually backlog entries for objects acquired between 2009 and 2014. The single largest backlog component was 2014 acquisitions (551 records), suggesting the system was severely behind before the 2015 data entry push.


The 2015 spike reflects a massive catch-up effort, not a surge in acquisitions. Staff member kryan entered 446 records and ieckert entered 457 records during 2015 — together accounting for 88% of that year's entries.

When we separate entry year from acquisition year, the true annual acquisition pace becomes visible: roughly 40–80 objects per year in 2009–2013, jumping to 568 in 2014, then settling to 100–240 per year from 2015 onward.

07 Additional Findings
Finding 01
The 2022 Gap

No records appear to have been entered in 2022, suggesting a possible staffing disruption, system migration, or pandemic-related delay. This gap is visible across all departments and warrants investigation.

Finding 02
Data Quality Issues

The AcquisitionYear extraction from ObjectNumber reveals malformed entries (values like 201, 457, 3, 6, 7, and blanks totaling ~44 records). Non-standard numbering or data entry errors requiring cleanup.

Finding 03
2014 Acquisition Surge

568 records acquired in 2014 represent the largest single acquisition year, almost entirely Architecture & Design. May reflect a major gift, bequest, or institutional acquisition.

Finding 04
Film is Emerging

A Film category (13 records) appeared in 2016–2019 and again in 2025, handled by tross and cshatzman. This may be a growing collection area worth monitoring and resourcing proactively.

Finding 05
2020–2021 COVID Anomaly

Only 2 records in 2020 (both Drawings & Prints by skang2) and 102 in 2021 (all Architecture & Design by swheeler). The pandemic likely disrupted operations, but swheeler's sustained A&D throughput in 2021 while every other department went dark highlights the fragility of single-person dependency.

08 Recommendations
Recommendation 01 — Immediate
Reassign Staff to Critical Departments

Assign dedicated cataloging staff to Media & Performance and Painting & Sculpture. Both departments have been effectively abandoned for 7–9 years.

Recommendation 02 — Near-Term
Photography Recovery Plan

Investigate why cataloging stopped in 2018 and develop a plan to resume. With 278 existing records, the department has a foundation but needs reactivation.

Recommendation 03 — Structural
Cross-Training Program

Extreme staff concentration risk means any single departure could halt an entire department's cataloging. Implement cross-training or rotation programs immediately.

Recommendation 04 — Data
Data Cleanup

Address the ~44 records with malformed acquisition years to ensure accurate historical reporting and improve data integrity across the collection.

Recommendation 05 — Ongoing
Set Annual Entry Targets

Set minimum annual entry targets per department to prevent the multi-year gaps seen in Media & Performance, Painting & Sculpture, and Photography.

Top Issue — 44 Malformed Acquisition Years

26 records end in ".201" (year truncated from 2010–2013). 12 records use non-standard formats (W-prefix, PS-prefix) with no extractable year. 6 records are loan/receipt numbers where a sequence number was read as a year. Fix: semi-automated for .201 records; manual TMS lookup for the rest.

Top Issue — 172 NULL Text Strings

Missing values stored as the literal text "NULL" rather than empty cells across Medium (35), Dimensions (63), CreditLine (32), DisplayDate (39), Dated (2). Standard blank checks won't catch these — reports currently overstate completeness. Fix: find-replace "NULL"→blank across affected columns.

P&S CreditLine Gap — 39% Missing

20 of 51 Painting & Sculpture records lack CreditLine — the worst rate of any field in any department. All 20 are from 2015 backlog entries by dking. Provenance for nearly half the department is unknown.

Film Completeness Crisis — 85–100% Missing

Film records (13 total) are missing Medium (85%), Dimensions (85%), and CreditLine (100%). The collection is essentially uncatalogued beyond basic identification. Establish mandatory fields before this category grows further.
